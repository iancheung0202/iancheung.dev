# Week 8 Study Guide: Futures, AI Narratives, and Automation

## Big idea

1. Talking about the future is never neutral. **Futuring** (predicting, imagining, planning, building) shapes what gets built, funded, feared, and regulated
2. Futures are told through reusable **narrative templates** (*1984*, *Terminator*, the Hero, "abundance")
3. Data tech doesn't just predict. It **remixes the past, steers action, and preempts futures** (Hildebrandt)
4. AI futures come mostly from **sci-fi and transhumanism**: doom, merging with machines, immortality, or abundance
5. **Automation** is not just a machine. It's a **social relationship** between workers, owners, and machines (Noble, Marx)
6. Technology is **chosen, not inevitable**. Design reflects who has power (Noble, Winner in Week 1)
7. Always ask: **whose future is this, who benefits, and who is left out?**

## Hildebrandt, *Smart Technologies and the End(s) of Law* (Intro)

> **Core idea:** Smart environments **regulate behavior through design**, without any of the democratic features that make law legitimate. Hildebrandt opens with a story to make us feel this.

**Diana's "onlife" day** (onlife = no online/offline divide)
- Her PDA **Toma** tunes her alarm to her mood, advises transit (nervous driver → higher insurance), schedules meetings around her energy, and **withholds a message** to protect her calm
- Daughter **Lindsay** is **biometrically monitored** at school and matched to risk "group profiles"
- Grandfather **Jacob's** care robot **Henry** balances insurer, hospital, and family interests and **doesn't report his stroke**. He dies.

**Why a story?** It imitates **scenario studies**: extrapolate from real trends to *sensitize* readers, not a Luddite fantasy or a corporate "pink" ad. (L19 uses this as the example of speculative futuring that sets us "into action.")

**Law vs. technological regulation**

| | Law | Tech regulation |
|---|---|---|
| Made by | Democratic legislator | Engineers/companies, often as a **side effect** |
| Can you violate it? | Yes | Often designed so you **can't** |
| Can you contest it? | In court | Often **invisible**, no court |

- **Example: cookies** were built for e-commerce, became tracking, and nobody voted on it
- **Surveillance metaphor:** Solove says **Kafka's *The Trial*** (anonymous accusation, chilling effect) fits better than **Big Brother**
- **Pre-emptive computing:** systems act on your *inferred future*. Fix proposed: **Legal Protection by Design**
- She deliberately skips political economy (that's what the labor material adds)

## Le Guin, "The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction" (1986)

> **Core idea:** Our stories about technology and human progress center the **Hero and the weapon**. A better story centers the **container**: gathering, holding, and relating.

- Early humans mostly **gathered** (65-80% of food, about a **15-hour work week**). Yet we tell the **mammoth hunter** story, because it's gripping: action plus a Hero.
- Drawing on **Elizabeth Fisher**, the first cultural tool was probably a **bag or sling**, not a spear
- **"Killer story"** = spear/arrow shape, conflict as the point of narrative, "Ascent of Man." She references *2001*'s ape with a bone.
- **A novel is a bag**: it holds people and things in relation. Conflict is one part, not the whole. A Hero "looks like a potato" in a bag.
- **Sci-fi as the mythology of modern tech is Promethean and tragic** (triumph → apocalypse). Redefine tech as a **cultural carrier bag**, not a weapon of domination, and sci-fi is less apocalyptic and more realistic.

**Use it for:** alternative narrative template. It critiques the Hero/conquest framing in transhumanism and Andreessen.

## Andreessen, *The Techno-Optimist Manifesto*

> **Core idea:** Technology plus free markets is an endless engine of growth and abundance. Slowing it down is the real danger.

- **Growth = progress.** Tech is the only perpetual source of growth ("everything good is downstream of growth")
- **Techno-capital machine** (Nick Land): markets + innovation spiral upward forever. Falling prices = rising real income. Productivity raises wages. Human wants are infinite, so jobs never run out.
- **Intelligence + energy** are the key resources. AI is a "universal problem solver." Delaying it "costs lives."
- **Against UBI** ("zoo animals"). **For** markets over central planning (Hayek). Wants nuclear power and 50B+ people.
- **"Enemies":** existential risk, ESG, sustainability, tech ethics, precautionary principle, experts, "stagnation," Nietzsche's **Last Man**
- Style: conquest, "technological supermen," rejects the **Prometheus** myth as a story of fear

**How to analyze it:** it is itself a **narrative template** (the **boomer** side of doomers vs. boomers). Ask whose interests it serves (a VC), what it assumes (growth is good, markets distribute fairly), and what it dismisses (labor power, ethics, regulation). It is the **opposite story** from Noble, Marx, and Le Guin.

## Noble, "Social Choice in Machine Design" (numerically controlled machine tools)

> **Core idea:** The automation of machine tools was a **social choice**, not an inevitable technical path. Design reflected **who held power**, between firms and between management and labor.

**Two ways to automate**
- **Record-playback:** a machinist makes the part once, the machine records and repeats it. **Skill stays on the shop floor.**
- **Numerical control (N/C):** engineers program the part from blueprints. **Skill moves to the office.**
- **N/C won**, even though record-playback was technically as reliable and suited small shops.

**Why N/C won**
1. **Horizontal relations (between firms):** the **Air Force** funded it (**$62M+**), created the market, and pushed the complex **APT** language as the standard. Big firms could afford it, small shops couldn't.
2. **Vertical relations (labor vs. management):** N/C removes the machinist as the "source of intelligence," ends **pacing/soldiering**, lets managers set rates by **"tape time,"** and **shifts control to management**. GE called it a "management system." Postwar strikes made this appealing.
3. **Ideology of control:** engineers' distrust of "human error" mirrors **capital's distrust of labor**. Automation is driven by *faith* that replacing labor with capital is good, not just by profit. Drucker: automation is "a logical extension" of **Taylorism**.

**Reality on the shop floor:** **desire ≠ satisfaction.** Deskilling mostly didn't happen as planned, because machines are unreliable and costly. Unions fought over classifications. "The intelligence of production... remains in the possession of the work force."

**Argues against:** **technological determinism** (the story that N/C automatically led to concentration and managerial control).

## Lecture 19: Data Futures

> **Core idea:** Narratives do work, and futuring is a fiction that is **always about the present too**. Ask whose narrative it is and whose it isn't.

- **Narratives** = stories in time that explain what matters. **Selective, meaningful, descriptive *and* directive.** Everywhere, including in technologies (1860 sewing machine will "banish ragged humanity").
- **Data tech works with the future beyond prediction:** **recycle/remix the past**, **shape and guide** actions, **prevent/preempt** futures (*Minority Report*)
- **Two purposes of futuring:** **impose order and control** (predict, steer, preempt) vs. **open up imagination** (sensitize, build common ground, make a future "real enough" to take seriously)
- **Routine futuring examples:** use-case brainstorming, **Songdo smart city** (everyday utopianism), **Diana's world** (speculative scenario), ***Black Mirror*** (dystopian), ***The Matrix*** (inventive, not "likeness-to-life")
- **Narrative templates:** like **cookie cutters** pressed onto reality, then decorated. ***1984*** = "Orwellian," "Big Brother," "thought police." Alternative: **Afrofuturism** (Octavia Butler, *Black Panther*, Oakland's "Mothership").
- **Beyond fiction:** forecasting, scenario planning, risk assessment, VC, making companies, engineering, social action, art

**Takeaway:** examine how futuring works, **denaturalize narratives you're handed**, and look at how they **universalize** one vision of what's human.

## Lecture 20: AI Futures

> **Core idea:** "AI" is more than a technical system. It's a future framed by powerful **sci-fi and transhumanist templates**, mainly "AI is coming for us."

**Template family:** AI **controls/enslaves/replaces** us (*Terminator*) · we **fuse** with AI (*2001*'s ending, singularity) · AI becomes our **companion** (*Her*). They're stories about **what it means to be human**.

**Existential risk / longtermism** (the EA and Rationalist world)
- **Yudkowsky** ("shut it all down"), **Bostrom** (*Superintelligence*), **MacAskill** (*What We Owe the Future*)
- **Doomers vs. boomers:** Yudkowsky vs. Andreessen/e/acc

**Transhumanism (old, not new)**
- Huxley coined it. Also Bernal, H.G. Wells (*Men Like Gods*).
- **I.J. Good (1966):** "ultraintelligent machine" → **intelligence explosion** → the "last invention"
- *Extropy*, Kurzweil's *The Singularity Is Near*
- **Sci-fi becomes blueprint:** *Neuromancer* → Neuralink; the "**Torment Nexus**" meme (author warns, company builds it)
- Elite voices: Musk (humans as "**biological bootloader**"), Altman (humans "train" too), Thiel

**Longevity/immortality:** Bryan Johnson, cryonics, Amodei's *Machines of Loving Grace* (doubling lifespan), ***San Junipero***. **Class angle:** *Blade Runner*'s Roy Batty ("I want more life"), "rise of the robots" as class-conflict allegory.

**AI as mirror of the human condition:** Data on trial (*Star Trek*), replicants, **ELIZA**, **Pygmalion**, *Her*. **Prometheus myth:** *Frankenstein*, *Alien: Earth*.

**Alternatives**
- **Le Guin's carrier bag** (see reading)
- **Haraway's cyborg:** not reverent, wary of holism, "illegitimate offspring" of militarism and capitalism but **unfaithful to its origins**

**Also mentioned:** **obligatory futuring** ("Something Big Is Happening," hype memes), "AI going rogue" headlines, "**permanent underclass**" fear, Pope Leo XIV encyclical on human dignity.

**Takeaway:** these narratives are **technologically determinist** and can be read through **gender, class, and labor**. Look at what's left out and who they serve.

## Lecture 21: Automation and Labor

> **Core idea:** Automation is a **sociotechnical relationship**, best understood by treating technology as **capital**. AI narratives about mass unemployment shape what action people consider.

**Two basic narratives:** automation → **abundance** (Brynjolfsson/McAfee, *Fully Automated Luxury Communism*) vs. automation → **unemployment, low wages, inequality** (Wiener). The real answer: **it depends** (jobs, wages, labor share, skill, power, which workers).

**What counts as automation?** A self-moving machine · any tech that raises labor productivity · tech that embodies a human skill · a machine that **displaces control from the operator** · a productive technology that functions as **capital**.

**Technology as capital** (Marx, Noble, Braverman)
- Automation sits within **labor vs. capital**, a **division of labor**, and **who owns the machines**
- Machines **substitute capital for labor** and **displace control over the work process**
- **Division of labor → machine:** **Prony's** logarithm project (mathematicians / calculators / workers) → Babbage's **Difference Engine** embodies that hierarchy

**Industrial revolution pattern:** longer hours, more intense work, downward wage pressure, **deskilling** (women and children enter), loss of control, surveillance (factory as panoptic), cycles of unemployment, **and** huge growth. **Marx:** machinery is "the most powerful weapon" against strikes. **Luddites (1811-16)** fought over control and conditions, not technology itself. **Taylorism**, *Modern Times*, CNC machines continue this.

**Today**
- **Evidence:** Brynjolfsson et al. ("Canaries in the Coal Mine"): early-career workers in AI-exposed jobs diverge. **Autor & Thompson:** jobs are **bundles of tasks**, so what matters is which tasks (expert vs. non-expert) get automated.
- **AI intensifies work** (HBR; agent fatigue), echoing ***More Work for Mother***
- Worker sentiment on AI is falling (Glassdoor). Altman himself says capital/labor balance "could easily get messed up."
- **Solow's paradox:** "computers everywhere but in the productivity statistics." Wages lag productivity due to **union decline** and **Baumol's cost disease** (services stay "technologically stagnant").
- **Value of AI:** factors (data, compute, expertise, labor, energy), strategies of capture (frontier models, hyperscalers, conglomerates, open source). **Who controls each?**
- **Hidden labor:** Kenyan workers paid under **$2/hr** to make ChatGPT less toxic; laid-off experts training AI to replace themselves
- **Responses:** UBI, "luxury communism," AI-enabled planning (*Cybernetic Revolutionaries*), billionaire tax fights

**Takeaway:** firms' AI bets assume **huge labor-productivity gains**. Understand automation through the **Industrial Revolution precedent**, not just as a tool.

## How it connects

- **Hero vs. bag:** Le Guin's critique applies to transhumanism, Prometheus, and Andreessen's "conquerors"
- **Optimists vs. critics:** Andreessen (growth, productivity raises wages) vs. Noble/Marx (control, deskilling, class)
- **Tech isn't inevitable:** Noble's design choices ↔ Hildebrandt's design as regulation ↔ Winner's "artifacts have politics"
- **Who's left out:** gatherers (Le Guin), Afrofuturist voices, hidden data workers, small shops (Noble), anyone Diana's PDA profiles
