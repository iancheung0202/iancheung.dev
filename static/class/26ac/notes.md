* Anglo-American Ballads (song that tells a dramatic/tragic story in 3rd person)
	* 4 lines per stanza in rhymed quatrains (abcb)
	* Strophic form (same music different stanzas)
	* Monophonic/homophonic
	* Guitar, banjo, mountain dulcimer
	* Barbar Allen (varying versions due to forgetfulness, misunderstanding of old words, localize names, censorship)
	* Child's Ballads (Francis Jamies Child)
	* Folk considered authentic
* Corridos (Mexican ballads that tell stories in 3rd person, derived from Spanish epic ballads/Romances)
	* 4 lines per verse, octosyllabic (8 syllables per line), abcb pattern
	* Strophic form
	* Two voices in parallel thirds
	* Simple I-IV-V progression
	* Voice Duets + Guitar (popular since cost-effective)
	* Conjunto (voice, accordion)
	* Mariachi (voice, trumpet, violin)
	* Documentation from community POV, cultural/political resistance, reinforce community bonds

* Blues
	* Recording industry vs music publishing (Tin Pan Alley)
	* ASCAP, copyright laws on written music only, Jim Crow Laws
	* Mamie Smith records "Crazy Blues" > genre as package and sell
	* Blues notes/scale/form (AAB, 12-bar)
	* Shuffle (swing) rhythm
	* Classic Blues / Race Records (used by industry to classify Black music to Black audience)
		* Female singer + piano / jazz combo
	* Rural Blues
		* Male singer + guitar/banjo (by singer)
	* Urban Blues
		* Male singer + electric guitar/harmonica + bass/piano/drums
* Bluegrass
	* Originated from Hillbilly Records (Race Records but marketed to rural white southerners) to Country Music
	* Bill Monroe created new genre marketed as "bluegrass" to distinguish
	* Mandolin/fiddle/banjo + bass/guiar + 1-4 SATB singers
	* Homophonic (heterophonic in jams)
	* Extremely fast with syncopation
	* Binary form (AABA, AABB, ABBA)
	* Take turns improvising
	* Considered "folk" even though commercially developed in the 1940s
	* Bluegrass festivals, structured improv (between rehearsal and performance), unspoken etiquette and social cues
* Rock n Roll
	* First used commercially by DJ Alan Freed towards white teenage audience (baby boom)
	* Emergence due to increased economic stability, end of segregation, television, teenager as an entity (race/region to generation marketing)
	* Cover versions (copyright laws, music appropriation)
	* MTV (new strat to promote music)
		* Michael Jackson's Thriller (changed white-only format)
