**Parametrized Curve:** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\frac{dy}{dt}}{\frac{dx}{dt}}\qquad \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx} \left( \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} \right) = \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{d}{dx} \left( \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} \right) \right) = \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{d/dt (dy/dt)}{dx/dt} \right)\qquad\text{Area} = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} y(t) x'(t) \, dt\qquad \text{Arc Length} = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt$
**Polar Coordinates:** $x = r \cos \theta, \quad y = r \sin \theta, \quad r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \quad \tan \theta = \frac{y}{x}\qquad\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} r^2 \, d\theta\qquad\text{Arc Length} = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{r^2 + \left( \frac{dr}{d\theta} \right)^2} \, d\theta$
**Vectors:** $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}| \cos \theta\qquad |\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}| = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}| \sin \theta\qquad\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0 \implies \text{orthogonal}\qquad\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{r_0} + t\mathbf{v}\qquad \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{r_1} - \mathbf{r_0}\qquad \text{proj}_{\mathbf{a}} \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|^2}\qquad \text{comp}_{\mathbf{a}} \mathbf{b} = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}|}$
**Derivatives:** $a(x-x_0) + b(y-y_0) + c(z-z_0) = 0\qquad D = \frac{|a x_1 + b y_1 + c z_1 + d|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}\qquad\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle f(t), g(t), h(t) \rangle \quad \mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle f'(t), g'(t), h'(t) \rangle \quad \int_a^b \mathbf{r}(t) \, dt = \left\langle \int_a^b f(t) \, dt, \int_a^b g(t) \, dt, \int_a^b h(t) \, dt \right\rangle \qquad\mathbf{T}(t) = \frac{\mathbf{r}'(t)}{|\mathbf{r}'(t)|}$
**Tangent Plane:** $z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0)\qquad$  **Linearization:** $L(x,y) - f(a,b) \approx f_x(a,b)(x - a) + f_y(a,b)(y - b)\qquad$ **Chain Rule:** $\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} \frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} \frac{dy}{dt}$. If $F(x,y) = 0$:           $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{F_x}{F_y}, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = -\frac{F_x}{F_z}, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = -\frac{F_y}{F_z}$
**Gradient:** $\nabla f = \langle f_x, f_y \rangle\qquad D_{\mathbf{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} = |\nabla f| \|\mathbf{u}\| \cos \theta = |\nabla f| \cos \theta\qquad$ **Tangent Plane to Level Surface:** $F_x(x_0, y_0, z_0)(x - x_0) + F_y(x_0, y_0, z_0)(y - y_0) + F_z(x_0, y_0, z_0)(z - z_0) = 0$
**Critical Points:** $f_x(a,b) = f_y(a,b) = 0 \qquad D(a,b) = f_{xx} f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2\qquad$ (If $D > 0$ and $f_{xx} > 0$: local minimum) (If $D > 0$ and $f_{xx} < 0$: local maximum) (If $D < 0$: saddle point) (If $D = 0$: inconclusive)
**Level Sets:** $f(x,y)=k\ \text{(curve) or } F(x,y,z)=k\ \text{(surface)}$$\qquad$ Direction of level curves: directional derivative is $0$), which is perpendicular to the gradient.
$\nabla f$ is **orthogonal** to the level curve $f=k$ and points in the direction of the **maximum rate of increase** $|\nabla f|$.  $\qquad \nabla F(x,y,z)$ is the **normal vector** to the tangent plane, thus, the **level set** $F=k$ at $P(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ 
**Continuity** *(Smooth functions like polynomials/exp/trig are continuous wherever defined)*: $f\ \text{is continuous at}\ (a,b)\ \text{if}$ $f(a,b)$ is defined, $\lim_{x,y \to a,b} f(x,y)$ exists, and $\lim f(x,y) = f(a,b)$
**Differentiability** *(Equivalent to existence of tangent plane)*: $f\ \text{is differentiable at}\ (a,b)\ \text{if } f(a+h, b+k) = f(a,b) + f_x(a,b)h + f_y(a,b)k + o(\sqrt{h^2 + k^2})$
**Lagrange Multipliers:** One Constraint: $\text{Solve } \nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\ \text{and}\ g(x, y, z) = k$. $\qquad$ Multiple Constraints: $\text{ Solve } \nabla f=\lambda\nabla g + \mu\nabla h \qquad g(x,y,z)=k_1 \qquad h(x,y,z)=k_2$
**Triple Integrals:** General: $\iiint_{E} f(x,y,z)dV$. Type I: $\iiint_{E} f(x,y,z)dV = \iint_{D} \left[ \int_{z=u_1(x,y)}^{z=u_2(x,y)} f(x,y,z) dz \right] dA$. Type II:  $\iiint_{E} f(x,y,z)dV = \iint_{D} \left[ \int_{x=u_1(y,z)}^{x=u_2(y,z)} f(x,y,z) dx \right] dA$. Type III: $\iiint_{E} f(x,y,z)dV = \iint_{D} \left[ \int_{y=u_1(x,z)}^{y=u_2(x,z)} f(x,y,z) dy \right] dA$
**Polar/Cylindrical Coordinates:** $\iint_R f(x, y)\ dy\ dx = \iint_D f(r \cos \theta, r \sin \theta)\ r\ dr\ d\theta\qquad x = r \cos \theta\qquad y = r \sin \theta\qquad z = z\qquad x^2 + y^2 = r^2\qquad dV = r\ dz\ dr\ d\theta$
**Spherical Coordinates:** $x = \rho \sin \phi \cos \theta\qquad y = \rho \sin \phi \sin \theta\qquad z = \rho \cos \phi\qquad x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = \rho^2 dx\ dy\ dz = \rho^2 \sin \phi\ d\rho\ d\phi\ d\theta$
**Center of Mass (Double/Triple Integral):** $M = \iint_D \rho(x, y)\ dA\qquad \bar{x} = \frac{1}{M} \iint_D x \rho(x, y)\ dA\qquad\bar{y} = \frac{1}{M} \iint_D y \rho(x, y)\ dA$ ($M_y = \iint_D x \rho(x, y)\ dA \Rightarrow \bar{x} = \frac{M_y}{M}$$\quad$ $M_x = \iint_D y \rho(x, y)\ dA \Rightarrow \bar{y} = \frac{M_x}{M}$)
**Change of Variable (Jacobian):** $J = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} = \begin{vmatrix} x_u & x_v \\ y_u & y_v \end{vmatrix} \qquad\iint_R f(x, y) dx dy = \iint_S f(x(u, v), y(u, v)) |J| du dv$
**Line Integral with respect to Arc Length ($ds$):** $\int_{C}f(x,y,z)ds=\int_{a}^{b}f(x(t),y(t),z(t))\sqrt{(\frac{dx}{dt})^{2}+(\frac{dy}{dt})^{2}+(\frac{dz}{dt})^{2}}dt\qquad\int_{C}f(x,y)ds=\int_{a}^{b}f(x(t),y(t))\sqrt{(\frac{dx}{dt})^{2}+(\frac{dy}{dt})^{2}}dt$
**Line Integral of a Scalar Function (with respect to coordinates):** $\int_{C}P~dx+Q~dy+R~dz=\int_{a}^{b}\left(P\frac{dx}{dt}+Q\frac{dy}{dt}+R\frac{dz}{dt}\right)dt\qquad\int_{C}f(x,y)dx=\int_{a}^{b}f(x(t),y(t)))\cdot x'(t)dt\qquad\int_{C}f(x,y)dy=\int_{a}^{b}f(x(t),y(t)))\cdot y'(t)dt$
**Line Integral of a Vector Field (Work Integral):** $\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t))\cdot \mathbf{r}^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{F}\cdot \mathbf{T} ds$
**Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals:** If $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f$, then $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = f(\mathbf{r}(b)) - f(\mathbf{r}(a))$. If $C$ is closed, $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$.
$\mathbf{F}$ is **conservative** if $\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$ is path-independent. Path-independent $\iff$ $\mathbf{F} = \nabla f$ for some scalar function $f$ (potential function). If $\mathbf{F} = P\mathbf{i} + Q\mathbf{j} + R\mathbf{k}\text{ is conservative} \iff \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x},\ \ \frac{\partial P}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial R}{\partial x},\ \ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial R}{\partial y}$ or $\mathbf{curl}~\mathbf{F}=0$
**Green's Theorem**: positively oriented **(counterclockwise)**, **closed** curve, region $D$ bounded by $C$: $\oint_{C}P~dx+Q~dy=\iint_{D}\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA$ .$\quad$ $\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$ if $\mathbf{F}$ is conservative ($Q_x=P_y$) $\iff \iint_{D}\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA=0$ 
**Curl of $\mathbf{F}$ (takes vector):** $\mathbf{curl}~\mathbf{F} = \nabla\times\mathbf{F}=\left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}\right)\mathbf{i}+\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial R}{\partial x}\right)\mathbf{j}+\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)\mathbf{k}$ (returns vector)
**Divergence of $\mathbf{F}$ (takes vector):** $\mathbf{div}~\mathbf{F} = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}=\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial Q}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial R}{\partial z}$ (returns scalar) $\qquad$ **Gradient of $\mathbf{f}$ (takes scalar):** $\mathbf{grad}~\mathbf{f} = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}=\langle f_x, f_y, f_z\rangle$ (returns vector) $\qquad\mathbf{curl}~(\mathbf{\nabla} f)=0 \qquad \mathbf{div}~(\mathbf{curl}~\mathbf{F})=0$
*Planes*: $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \mathbf{P}_0 + u\mathbf{a} + v\mathbf{b}$ ($\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are non-parallel vectors in the plane) | *Explicit*: $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \langle u, v, f(u, v) \rangle$ (if $z = f(x, y)$, $u=x$, $v=y$) | *Spherical*: $\mathbf{r}(\rho, \theta, \phi) = \langle \rho \sin \phi \cos \theta, \rho \sin \phi \sin \theta, \rho \cos \phi \rangle$ (use if $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = \rho^2$) 
*Cone*: $\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \langle u \cos v, u \sin v, u \rangle$ (use if $z = \pm \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$) | *Cylinder*: $\mathbf{r}(\theta, z) = \langle \rho \cos \theta, \rho \sin \theta, z \rangle$ (use if $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$  and $\rho$ is a constant radius)
**Surface Integral of a Scalar Function (Surface Area Element $dS$):** $A(S) = \iint_{D} |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| \, dA \quad \text{if parameterized by } \mathbf{r}(u, v) \text{ OR } = \iint_{D} \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2 + 1} \, dA \quad \text{if } z=f(x, y) \text{ (Explicit function)}$
$\iint_{S} f(x, y, z) \, dS = \iint_{D} f(\mathbf{r}(u, v)) |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| \, dA \quad \text{OR} \quad \iint_{D} f(x, y, g(x, y)) \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2 + 1} \, dA\quad \text{OR} \quad \iint_{D} f(x, h(x,z), z) \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial z}\right)^2 + 1} \, dA\quad \text{OR} \quad \iint_{D} f(j(y,z), y, z) \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial z}\right)^2 + 1} \, dA$
**Surface Integral of a Vector Field (Flux Integral $d\mathbf{S}$):** $\iint_{S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_{D} \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u, v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v) \, dA\quad\text{ OR }\quad \iint_{S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_{D} \left( -P \frac{\partial g}{\partial x} - Q \frac{\partial g}{\partial y} + R \right) \, dA$ ($z=g(x,y)$, upward orientation (normal vector points up), and $\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangle$)
**Stokes' Theorem:** $\oint_{C} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_{S} \mathbf{curl}\, \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$, orientation of $C$ (boundary curve) and $\mathbf{n}$ (normal vector of $S$) must follow the **Right-Hand Rule**.
**Divergence Theorem (for closed surface, oriented outward normal vector):** $\iint_{S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_{E} \mathbf{div}\, \mathbf{F} \, dV = \iiint_{E} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV$
